When floodwater in a Sand Springs home is an emergency.

Floodwater in a living space is an emergency from the first inch. The dispatch line is open 24/7, nights and weekends included, and a crew can start planning the pump-out as soon as you describe where the water came from. While you wait, stay out of standing water if an outlet, cord or appliance is under it, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas concern before you call us.

Shut off the main breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Lift photos, paperwork, medications and electronics to a dry shelf, and keep children and pets out of the affected rooms. Do not run a household vacuum on wet floors.

How flooding reaches homes in Sand Springs.

Sand Springs sits between the Arkansas River and a web of creeks, so flood water arrives from more than one direction. The city's floodplain management plan names Fisher Creek and its tributary Anderson Creek as the flood sources behind the Meadow Valley subdivision, where the plan still counts 20 repetitive loss structures after a buyout program completed in 2005 removed 28 previously flooded structures. If your home is near either creek, a short and hard rain can matter as much as a river rise.

The river adds a second path. The Army Corps of Engineers reported Keystone Dam releases of about 275,000 cubic feet per second in 2019, the highest since 1986, and engineers watched the levee system that runs about 20 miles from Sand Springs to Tulsa. The practical step is to look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center before the next wet spring and to ask the city Neighborhood Services office which floodplain rules apply to repairs at your address.

Why flood water is handled as Category 3.

Water that rises from the ground has crossed yards, roads and ditches before it reaches a floor, so restoration standards treat it as grossly contaminated even when it looks clear. That classification drives what stays and what goes. The table shows how the three water categories compare.

Water categories and what each means for the cleanup.
Category.Typical source.What it means.Usual handling.
Category 1Broken supply line, clean tap waterClean at the source and it degrades over timeExtract and dry, most materials can stay.
Category 2Washing machine overflow, dishwasher dischargeCarries contamination that can make people illExtract, clean, remove soaked padding.
Category 3Floodwater, sewage, river or creek overflowGrossly contaminated by outside materialRemove porous materials, clean and treat, dry the structure.

The flood cleanup sequence, step by step.

  1. Walk the property for safety, locate the main breaker and note any gas appliance that sat in water.
  2. Remove standing water with submersible pumps for depth and extraction units for the film left on the floor.
  3. Take out porous materials that floodwater soaked, including carpet and pad, insulation, baseboards and drywall cut above the water line.
  4. Clean and treat the surfaces that stay with products suited to Category 3 contamination.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and log moisture meter and thermal camera readings until framing and subfloor match dry reference readings.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and it is reasonable to ask any company whether its crews work to it. Daily readings are the proof that drying is finished, so ask to see them.

Flood, storm or plumbing: which water do you have.

If rain came through a damaged roof or window rather than rising from the ground, the storm water damage cleanup page describes that job. When the water came up through a floor drain, sewage backup cleanup covers the safety steps. A lower level that flooded from seepage or a failed sump pump belongs on the basement water damage page, and the drying stage for every one of them is explained under structural drying and dehumidification.

Crews also work across the river in west Tulsa, and the same 24/7 line serves both sides of the Arkansas.

What decides the cost of flood cleanup.

No two flood jobs share a number, so scope and price are explained before work starts. These five factors move the total the most.

  • Water categoryFloodwater is Category 3, which adds removal of porous materials and treatment..
  • Affected areaSquare footage and the number of rooms and floors the water reached..
  • Materials involvedCarpet, pad, drywall, insulation, cabinets and flooring each carry a different remove or save decision..
  • Drying daysEquipment count and days on site follow how wet the structure got and how thick the materials are..
  • Rebuild scopeCleanup and drying are one scope. Replacing drywall, trim and flooring is another..

Flood insurance is a separate policy.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department states that flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy and that flood protection requires additional insurance. Check your policy for a flood endorsement or a National Flood Insurance Program policy before you call your agent.

Contact the agent right away if you were flooded, take photos of the damage, make reasonable temporary repairs and keep receipts.

If you live in Sand Springs, read the notes for the Meadow Valley neighborhood, which the city names in its floodplain management plan.
