Where Meadow Valley fits in Sand Springs.

Meadow Valley is a subdivision inside the Sand Springs city limits, and the City of Sand Springs names it in its floodplain management plan. The plan places an early warning siren inside the subdivision and says the city held a flood preparedness meeting for Meadow Valley residents and property owners.

The plan also describes a study by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers of flooding along Fisher Creek as it affects the subdivision. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. This page does not say that every Meadow Valley home sits in a mapped flood zone, and it does not say that your home does.

What happened in May 2019.

Storms dropped 10 to 16 inches of rain on most of northeastern Oklahoma in late May 2019, according to reporting by StateImpact Oklahoma. NewsOn6 reported on May 31, 2019 that some houses in the Meadow Valley neighborhood had feet of flood water inside.

That is one documented event. It is not a promise that your street floods, and it is not a reason to assume your house is safe. It is a reason to know your plan before water arrives.

Why the source of the water matters.

According to ANSI/IICRC S500, water from rivers and streams is generally treated as Category 3, because it can carry soil, sewage and chemicals. A supply line break starts cleaner, but clean water can be reclassified as it sits and picks up contaminants.

That is why a crew asks where the water came from before it asks anything else. The answer sets what can be dried in place and what has to come out. Read more on flood damage cleanup and storm water cleanup.

Safe first steps before anyone arrives.

  • Stay out of water that is moving, deep or touching outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  • Turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If you cannot, call your electric utility.
  • Treat floodwater and sewage as contaminated. If you must enter, wear boots, gloves and eye protection and keep children and pets out.
  • Do not run fans or a household vacuum on sewage or floodwater. A household vacuum is not built for wet pickup and can spread contamination.
  • Photograph every wet room before anything is moved, and keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them.
  • Do not assume wet drywall, flooring or insulation can be saved or must be thrown out. That call is made after moisture readings, and nobody can promise the outcome in advance.

What the work looks like.

After the source is stopped or identified, a crew pulls standing water with water extraction, removes materials that cannot be dried and sets air movers and dehumidifiers for structural drying. Readings are taken at each visit so the drying decision rests on numbers.

When sewage is involved, the cleanup follows a different plan, described on the sewage backup page.

Insurance and documentation.

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and flood coverage is typically a separate policy. According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, homeowners should contact their agent right away after a loss. This page cannot tell you what your policy covers.

Ask the crew for moisture readings and a written drying log, and give them to your adjuster with your photos.
