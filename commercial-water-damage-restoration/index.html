A water loss at a business is a revenue problem too.

When water reaches a storefront, an office suite or a tenant unit, every hour of downtime costs money on top of the damage. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights and weekends, so a Friday evening pipe failure does not sit until Monday. Before the crew arrives, shut off the water supply to the area, cut power to wet circuits only if the panel is dry, and move inventory, records and electronics off the floor.

If water is dripping through light fixtures or near electrical gear, keep people out and call the utility or 911 first. Take photos of everything before it is moved.

In a downtown district such as the Box District, where restaurants and shops sit side by side, one tenant's leak can become the neighbor's loss. Sorting out which unit the water started in early helps the landlord and each tenant's insurer, so write down the time you found it and who shut the water off.

Working around occupied space.

Most commercial jobs cannot close the building for a week. The crew can section off the affected area with containment barriers, run extraction and drying equipment in the wet zone and keep the rest of the floor usable. After-hours work is available for businesses that cannot be disrupted during opening hours.

Equipment placement matters in a public space. Air movers and dehumidifiers are loud and need power, so the crew lays out cords and units to keep aisles, exits and entrances clear.

Drying a commercial structure properly.

Moisture meters and thermal imaging map the wet areas, and the crew builds a drying plan around what is wet, including ceiling tile, wall cavities, slab and flooring. The mechanics are the same ones described under structural drying and dehumidification, scaled to the square footage and the building's ventilation.

If the water arrived from a rising creek or storm instead of a pipe, read the flood damage cleanup page and the storm water damage page for how those sources change the plan.

Landlords, tenants and insurers: who needs what.

Commercial property policies, tenant liability policies and landlord policies all respond differently, so each party should read its own policy and call its own insurer. Typical paperwork includes dated photos, a moisture map, an inventory of damaged contents and the extraction and drying logs.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while long-term leaks and maintenance failures often are not. Ask your insurer which category your loss falls in before any rebuild decision is made.

What to do before the crew arrives at a business.

  • Shut off the water and, if it is safe, power to the affected area.
  • Move inventory, paperwork and electronics off the floor.
  • Tell staff and customers which areas to avoid.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time you discovered it.
  • Call your commercial insurer and the building owner or manager.

According to ANSI/IICRC S500, drying is guided by measured moisture readings, which is why a business claim should carry daily readings and logs.
