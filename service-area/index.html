How coverage is decided for each address.

Three things decide whether a crew can reach you quickly: where the nearest crew is when you call, how far you are from the main roads and what the weather is doing. Storm nights are the busiest, and the dispatcher will tell you honestly if the window is longer than usual.

Call as early as you can. A call placed while water is still shallow gets a faster plan than one placed after the wet area has spread.

What to have ready.

  • Your street address and the nearest crossroad.
  • A description of the source and whether it is stopped.
  • Photos of the damage, if you can take them safely.
  • Your insurance carrier and policy number, if you have them handy.
  • A phone number for someone who will be on site.

Routes crews use from Sand Springs.

From the Sand Springs base, crews reach Tulsa by US Highway 412 and the Keystone Expressway. State Highway 51 leads west toward Mannford, and State Highway 97 runs south through Prattville. Oakhurst sits along Interstates 44 and 244, and Kiefer is reached by State Highway 67 and US Highway 75A. Sperry is north of downtown Tulsa.

Construction, crashes and weather can change those routes, so the dispatcher will tell you the window on the day you call, not a fixed number.

When storms are the reason you are calling.

Severe weather brings many calls at once. If your situation is stable, such as a slow drip or a stain that has not grown, say so, because crews are sent first to jobs where water is actively spreading. If you can safely stop the source or cover an opening, do it and tell the dispatcher what you did.

According to the 2020 Census, Tulsa had about 413,000 residents, Kiefer 2,187, Mannford 3,262 and Sperry 1,115. The scale differs a great deal, and so does the way crews reach each place.

Towns outside the five pages.

Catoosa, Coweta, Bristow, Jenks and Glenpool do not have their own pages, but crews do work there when the schedule allows. Tell the dispatcher the town, the road and the nearest landmark. If the trip is long, the dispatcher will tell you how long and offer an honest window.

Tulsa, Mannford, Oakhurst, Sperry and Kiefer each have a page with ZIP codes and local notes. Those pages are the best place to check before you call, and they link to the services most often needed in each area.

Nearby places people ask about.

People in the Prattville area, the Box District, The Triangle and near Case Community Park all reach the same dispatch line. Neighbors along Keystone Lake in Mannford and along the interstates in Oakhurst do too. If you are between two towns, call and describe where you are.
