Call now.

A bulging ceiling is an emergency you can act on tonight.

A ceiling that bulges, drips or sags is holding water in the drywall, and drywall full of water can fall without warning. Dispatch is open 24/7, so call at any hour. Move people and furniture out from under the sag, then puncture a small hole at the lowest point of a bulge only if you can place a bucket and stand clear, because trapped water releases all at once.

Turn off the lights and fans on any circuit near the wet ceiling. If water is dripping through a fixture, stay away from it and call the utility or 911 before the restoration line.

Find what is above the ceiling.

Ceiling stains tell you that water is arriving, but not from where. The usual sources sit in a short list, and each one has its own fix.

Roof or flashing.

Water follows decking and rafters before it shows on the ceiling, often a few feet from the actual hole.

Upstairs bathroom.

A failed supply line, tub overflow or toilet seal drips down through the floor and onto the ceiling below.

Attic HVAC.

A clogged condensate drain or a cracked pan sends water through the ceiling under the unit.

Supply or drain line.

A leak inside the joist space can run for weeks before it stains the paint.

Roof leaks: from the shingle to the drywall.

Roof leaks have no page of their own here because they end up the same way, as a wet ceiling. Water gets through a failed flashing, a lifted shingle or a damaged vent boot, soaks the decking and insulation, and shows up as a stain that grows after each rain. Fixing the ceiling without fixing the roof only repeats the damage.

Oklahoma storms bring wind, hail and heavy rain, and tree limbs can open a roof in seconds. The city's Neighborhood Services page says a roof repair needs a permit only when the decking beneath is replaced, and that roofing companies must hold a state license. Ask the roofer which applies before work starts, and call the line here for the wet ceiling and insulation below it.

A tarp over the opening limits further damage until a roofer can repair it. If tree damage caused the opening, the storm water damage cleanup page covers the broader cleanup.

How the ceiling is dried and repaired.

The crew opens the ceiling where the water is trapped, removes wet insulation and drywall, and measures the rest with a moisture meter and thermal camera. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the cavity before anything closes it up. The same drying process is described under structural drying and dehumidification.

If water ran down the wall below the ceiling, the wall and drywall repair page covers what happens next. Stained but dry drywall can sometimes be sealed and repainted, while sagging or soft drywall is cut out and replaced.
