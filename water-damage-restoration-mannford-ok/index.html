Around-the-clock dispatch for Mannford homes.

Mannford is in Creek County, with a 2020 population of 3,262 and ZIP code 74044. It lies in the Salt Creek valley on the southwest arm of Keystone Lake, which shapes how water behaves on local lots. Our crews head west from Sand Springs on State Highway 51, and the emergency line is answered 24/7.

Tell the dispatcher your road, how deep the water is and whether it came from a pipe, a storm or the lake. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained first.

Why lake and creek homes need a different plan.

Homes near Salt Creek and the lake often sit on sloped or low-lying ground, where runoff and groundwater reach crawl spaces and basements. Water that comes up from the ground carries silt and bacteria, so it is treated differently from a clean supply-line leak. The crawl space page and the basement page describe those cases.

If lake water or creek water entered the building, the flood damage page applies. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so ask your agent about it before the next storm.

What to do while the crew is on the way.

Close the main valve.

If a pipe broke, shut off the water at the main before anything else.

Turn off power to wet areas.

Do it only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Photograph the water line.

Take pictures of walls and furniture at the highest water mark.

Keep children and pets out.

Floodwater and sewage can carry bacteria.

Mannford water system and service lines.

The Mannford Public Works Authority is replacing 992 meters with advanced metering, according to the Oklahoma Water Resources Board in 2026. A new meter does not stop a pipe from bursting, but a rising bill after the change can be a clue to a slow leak. If your meter spins with every fixture off, you have a leak somewhere on the line.

A plumber finds and repairs the leak, and the restoration crew dries what it soaked. The burst pipe page explains how those two jobs fit together.

ZIP code 74044 and nearby roads.

Our Mannford coverage centers on ZIP code 74044. If you live on a road that crosses into a neighboring ZIP, call and ask whether a crew can reach you. Sand Springs is the base, and travel time on State Highway 51 depends on traffic and weather.

Boat docks, lake houses and rural properties sometimes need extra access planning, so mention gates, long drives and low bridges when you call.

Seasonal and vacation homes near the lake.

Homes around Keystone Lake are often used part of the year. A pipe that freezes or a supply line that fails in an empty house can run for days before anyone notices. If you own a lake house, shut off the main when you leave, keep the heat on in winter and ask a neighbor to check on it after storms.

When you call about a lake property, mention gates, long driveways, steep grades and whether the house is accessible by a regular road in wet weather. Crews can plan the right vehicle and equipment when they know the access in advance.

Insurance and flood questions for Mannford.

Mannford sits close to Keystone Lake and Salt Creek, so flood coverage is worth a conversation with your agent. The Oklahoma Insurance Department states that flood is not covered by a standard homeowners policy and needs separate coverage. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for an address.

Whatever the cause, photograph the damage, call your agent right away and make reasonable temporary repairs. Keep every receipt, because emergency costs are often part of a claim.
