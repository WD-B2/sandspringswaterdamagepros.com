Who we are and what we do.

Sand Springs Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration service based at 700 E Charles Page Blvd in Sand Springs, Oklahoma. We take calls 24/7 for burst pipes, appliance leaks, storm water, sewage backups, floods and the drying that follows. The work covers homes first, with commercial buildings handled on their own page.

We do not claim awards, ratings or customer counts on this site. What we offer is a phone that is answered at any hour, an explanation of the scope and price before work starts and drying that is measured with readings.

How a job runs.

A call starts with questions about the source, the location and the safety of the space. The crew then extracts water, maps moisture and sets equipment. Each visit includes readings, and the job ends when the structure matches a dry reference. Our structural drying page explains the method.

Where we work.

We serve Sand Springs and the nearby towns of Tulsa, Mannford, Oakhurst, Sperry and Kiefer. The services page lists every service, and the homepage gives the overview.

What we do not do.

We do not repair plumbing, replace water heaters, tarp roofs or fix drainage. Those jobs belong to licensed trades, and we work alongside them. We also do not give coverage opinions. Your policy and your insurer decide that, and we keep the records that help.

Why the site talks about insurance so often.

Most water damage questions are really coverage questions in disguise. People want to know whether the loss will be paid for, and the answer depends on the cause. We cite the Oklahoma Insurance Department wherever we discuss coverage, because it is a neutral public source, and we keep moisture readings, photographs and drying logs for every job so that you have records to hand to your insurer.

According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, homeowners should call their agent right away after a loss, photograph the damage and make reasonable temporary repairs. We build our own instructions around that advice.

We do not tell you what your policy covers. That is between you and your insurer. What we can do is describe the work, explain the scope and price and give you the paperwork that shows what was done.

How the content on this site is built.

Local facts on this site come from public sources such as the City of Sand Springs, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, the National Weather Service and the Oklahoma Insurance Department. Where we could not confirm a detail from a primary source, we left it out. For example, we do not name the water utility for every nearby town, because that depends on your address.

The guides on the blog follow the same rule. They explain how water damage works, what insurers usually do and what to ask any contractor, whether or not you call us.

What the first call sounds like.

The dispatcher asks four things in order. What happened, and is it still happening? Where is the water, and how deep? Does it touch electrical equipment? What is the address and the best number to reach you? From those answers the dispatcher decides how many people to send, what equipment to load and how long the trip will take.

You will also hear safety instructions. They usually include closing the main valve, staying out of water near outlets and photographing the damage. Those steps protect you and make the first hour of the job more efficient.

Types of property we handle.

  • Single-family houses, including older homes with plaster and newer homes with open plans.
  • Duplexes, apartments and rental houses where landlord and tenant both need records.
  • Small commercial spaces such as shops and offices, covered on the commercial water damage page.
  • Barns, shops and outbuildings on rural lots near the edges of our service area.

Each type of property dries differently. A slab-on-grade ranch, a house over a crawl space and a two-story home with a finished basement all need different plans, and the first visit exists to sort out which one you have.

What to do before you call us.

  • Stop the source if you can do it safely.
  • Keep everyone out of water that touches outlets or the panel.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.

A note on honesty.

Nobody can promise an exact arrival time in a storm, an exact drying time in a wall nobody has opened or an exact insurance result. We say what we know, what we do not and what we will check. If a situation is outside our work, such as a failed water heater or a leaking roof, we say so and point you to the right trade.

That candor is part of how we expect to be judged. A short, accurate answer on the phone is worth more to a person standing in water than a confident answer that falls apart on site.

Hours and how to reach us.

Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available. The base is at 700 E Charles Page Blvd in Sand Springs, and the contact page has the map.
