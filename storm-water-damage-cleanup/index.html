How storm water gets in.

Roof damage.

Missing shingles, lifted flashing or a fallen limb let rain through the decking.

Windows and doors.

Wind-driven rain gets under sills, frames and seals.

Walls.

Hail or debris punctures siding and sheathing.

Grade.

Runoff flows in at low doors, garages and basement window wells.

Roof leaks that soak ceilings are covered in the ceiling water damage repair page. When runoff or river water flooded at ground level, the flood damage page is the better fit.

Storm damage and insurance language.

Insurance treats storm water differently depending on how it entered. The Oklahoma Insurance Department says standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and exclude interior stormwater damage when there was no damage to the roof or walls. Rain that comes through a storm-damaged roof is a different case from rain that seeped under a door.

Document the roof or wall opening, the interior water and the temporary repairs you made. Contact your agent right away, and keep receipts for tarps and board-up.

Sand Springs storm context.

Sand Springs sits along the Arkansas River and Keystone Lake, and the area has heavy spring thunderstorms. NWS Tulsa's 1991 to 2020 climate normal puts March precipitation at 3.10 inches, and storms often bring wind-driven rain. Keystone Dam released about 275,000 cfs in May 2019, according to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, which is a reminder that river flooding and roof leaks are different problems.

Roof repair that replaces decking requires a permit with the City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office, and roofing companies need a state license. Ask your roofer about both.

After the water is out: what to inspect.

Once standing water is gone, look at ceilings, attic insulation, window frames and the base of exterior walls. Water travels along rafters and down wall cavities, so a stain may show up in a different room than the leak. Wet insulation in the attic loses its insulating value and can hold moisture against the roof deck for weeks.

If you can reach the attic safely, check the underside of the roof deck for dark stains, damp insulation and daylight. Photograph anything you find before it is moved.

Hail, wind and the second storm.

Hail and wind damage may not leak right away. A cracked shingle or bent flashing can hold through one rain and fail in the next. After a severe storm, have a roofer inspect the roof even if nothing has come inside yet, and document the findings with your insurer.

What to do before the crew arrives after a storm.

  • Stay away from downed power lines and wet electrical panels.
  • Do not walk under a ceiling that is sagging or dripping heavily.
  • Place containers under drips and move valuables to a dry room.
  • Do not climb on a wet roof.
  • Photograph the damage inside and outside.
