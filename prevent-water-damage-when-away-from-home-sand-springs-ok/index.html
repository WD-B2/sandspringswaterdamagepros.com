How to Prevent Water Damage When Away From Home in Sand Springs, OK.

A pre-trip checklist for Sand Springs homeowners who will be gone for a weekend, a winter or a season.

Written by the Sand Springs Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(918) 306-8113.
Call now.

A home left empty is a home with no one to notice a drip. A small leak that you would catch in an hour can run for days, and a burst line can empty a tank into the floors. The risk is highest in winter and on long trips, but it exists any time you are gone.

These steps take about thirty minutes and cover most of the risk.

Before you leave.

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, then close it.
  3. Turn your water heater to a vacation setting or off.
  4. Check washing machine hoses and replace rubber ones with braided stainless hoses.
  5. Clear gutters and extend downspouts.

If you cannot shut off the main, at least close the valves to the washing machine, the dishwasher and the ice maker.

Winter trips.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department notes that frozen pipe damage may not be covered if the home was left unheated and the damage came from negligence. Keep the heat on and set the thermostat to a safe level. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls and disconnect outdoor hoses.

Read about frozen pipe damage for the cleanup after a break.

Summer trips.

In summer the main risks are AC condensate lines and appliances. Check that the condensate drain flows. Make sure the float switch works and that the drain pan is clear. The AC leak page explains what happens when it fails.

Arrange a house check.

Ask a neighbor, a friend or a house sitter to walk through every few days. Give them the location of the main shutoff and your contact details. A quick look at ceilings and floors catches problems early.

Smart leak sensors and automatic shutoff valves send alerts to your phone and can close the water supply. A plumber can install them. They are an investment that pays off if a pipe bursts when you are away.

What to leave behind.

  • The main shutoff location, labeled and written down.
  • A list of emergency numbers, including a plumber and a restoration crew.
  • Your insurance agent's contact information.
  • The location of the electrical panel.

Sand Springs details worth knowing.

The city water page lists an after-hours emergency line for utility problems. Save it before you leave. The system serves about 12,000 customers across roughly 150 square miles, so a leak on the street side of the meter is the utility's job, while a leak inside the house is yours.

For storm season, the National Weather Service Tulsa office publishes forecasts, and March precipitation averages 3.10 inches for 1991 to 2020. Heavy storms can arrive while you are gone, so clear gutters first.

If you come home to water.

Do not walk in water near outlets. Shut off the main, take photos and call. The 24/7 line is (918) 306-8113. See Tulsa and Mannford pages for our service area, and the appliance leak page if a hose or valve was the cause.

A room-by-room walk-through before you leave.

  • Kitchen: check under the sink, the dishwasher hose and the refrigerator water line.
  • Bathrooms: look at toilet supply lines, under vanities and around the tub.
  • Laundry: replace old rubber hoses and make sure the drain hose is secure.
  • Utility area: look at the water heater, the pan beneath it and the softener.
  • Outside: disconnect hoses, check gutters and make sure downspouts drain away.

Take a few photographs of each area before you go. If something looks different when you return, the pictures show what changed.

Rental properties and second homes.

Vacant rentals and seasonal houses are at higher risk because nobody lives there to notice a leak. Consider an automatic shutoff valve with leak sensors, a smart thermostat that alerts you to temperature drops and a scheduled visit from a property manager or neighbor. A burst pipe in an empty house can run for days.

According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, frozen pipe damage may not be covered if a home was left unheated, which makes a thermostat setting one of the most important decisions before a winter trip.

Cost of prevention compared with cleanup.

Braided stainless washing machine hoses, a few foam pipe sleeves and a leak sensor cost little compared with the deductible alone on a water claim. An automatic shutoff valve costs more, and it is worth considering for a second home or any house that sits empty for weeks.

Thirty minutes before the trip is worth more than any amount of cleanup after it.
