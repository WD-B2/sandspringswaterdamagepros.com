A pipe has burst: do this first.

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons in an hour, so speed matters. Dispatch runs 24/7, nights and weekends included. Shut off the main water valve first, then open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the line, and call us while the crew is on the way.

If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and shut off power only if the panel is dry. Move valuables off the floor and photograph the damage before cleanup begins.

Why pipes burst.

  • Freezing water expanding inside a line, covered under the frozen pipe page.
  • Old galvanized or polybutylene pipe that corroded or failed at a fitting.
  • High water pressure that stressed joints and fixtures.
  • A shifted slab or foundation that pulled on a line.
  • A damaged fitting or a failed connector under a sink or toilet.

Freeze damage has its own page at frozen pipe water damage, and a leak under the foundation is covered under slab leak water damage. A plumber repairs the pipe, and the restoration crew removes water and dries the structure.

The dry-out after a burst pipe.

Crews extract standing water, remove materials that cannot be dried, such as wet insulation and soft drywall, and set air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture meters and a thermal camera confirm where water went inside walls. Because the break may be above the wet area, the crew also checks the ceilings and floors above and below.

The water extraction page explains how the first hours work, and the structural drying page covers the equipment.

Documentation for your insurer.

Take photos before anything is moved, keep the broken pipe section if the plumber removes it and save the plumbing invoice. Mitigation logs and moisture readings from the restoration crew support the claim. Insurers often ask for proof that water was stopped and drying began promptly.

Hidden bursts: the slow version.

Not every burst announces itself with a spray of water. A pinhole in a copper line or a cracked fitting inside a wall can leak for days. Signs include a sudden jump in the water bill, the sound of water when everything is off, a warm or damp spot on a wall and paint that bubbles for no clear reason. A meter test, where you shut every fixture off and watch the meter, tells you whether water is moving.

If the meter is running and you cannot see the source, shut off the main and call a plumber and a restoration crew. Drying cannot begin until the leak is stopped, and the crew can map where the water has traveled while the plumber looks for the break.

According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, homeowners should contact their agent right away after a loss and make reasonable temporary repairs to prevent further damage.
