Warm spots, running water sounds or a high water bill?

A slab leak rarely announces itself with a flood. The usual signs are a hot spot on the floor, the sound of water running when everything is off, a water bill that jumps with no change in use, damp carpet along a wall or baseboards that swell for no reason. If you see these, shut off the main water valve and call.

Dispatch runs 24/7, nights and weekends included. A quick way to check is to turn off every fixture and watch the water meter. If it still moves, water is going somewhere.

What a slab leak is.

Many homes have supply lines or drain lines in or below the concrete foundation. When one of them corrodes, shifts or splits, water collects under the slab, rises through cracks and wets the flooring above. A plumber finds the leak with acoustic or thermal equipment and repairs or reroutes the line.

The damage a slab leak causes.

Water rises through the concrete and soaks carpet, pad, tile grout and the bottom of the walls. Hardwood cups and baseboards swell. Over time, soil under the slab can wash out and the slab can crack.

The wet flooring is handled like other surfaces, and the carpet page and the hardwood page describe the specific cases. The underlying line is handled as in the burst pipe page.

How restoration fits with plumbing repairs.

  1. Shut off the water and confirm with the meter.
  2. A plumber locates and repairs or reroutes the line.
  3. The restoration crew removes wet flooring that cannot be dried.
  4. Air movers, dehumidifiers and drying mats dry the slab surface and walls.
  5. Readings confirm the slab and walls are dry before new flooring goes in.

A plumber's work may involve cutting or breaking the slab, which requires coordination with the drying plan. Ask both trades to share their schedule.

Insurance and slab leaks.

Read your policy.

Coverage for slab leaks depends on the policy and the cause. Sudden and accidental discharge is often covered, while wear, corrosion and gradual leaks often are not. Some policies treat the cost to reach the pipe separately.

Why slab leaks happen.

Pipes under a slab can fail because of corrosion, abrasion against concrete, shifting soil or a poor installation. Hot water lines often fail first because heat speeds up wear. Once a pinhole forms, the pressure pushes water into the soil and up through cracks, where it appears as dampness, warmth or a rising bill.

A plumber can reroute the line through walls or attic space, repair it in place by breaking the slab or replace it by lining. Each option has a different cost and disruption, so ask for the choices in writing.

According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, homeowners should contact their agent right away and document the damage, which is especially important when the leak is found by a plumber after the fact.
