The Water Damage Remediation Process in Sand Springs, OK.

A stage-by-stage walkthrough of what happens after you call, so nothing on site comes as a surprise.

Written by the Sand Springs Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(918) 306-8113.
Call now.

People call a restoration company on one of the worst days of their year. Knowing what is about to happen makes it easier to make decisions and to spot a crew that is cutting corners. Remediation is a process with stages, and each stage has a purpose.

The steps below follow the general pattern described by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Every house is different, so some stages are short and others take days.

Stage one: the call and the triage.

The dispatcher asks what happened, where the water is, how long it has been there and whether it touches electrical panels. Those answers decide how many people come and what equipment goes on the truck. Be ready with the address, access details and whether the source is stopped.

Stage two: inspection and categorizing the water.

On arrival the crew checks the source, the safety of the space and the type of water. Water is classed into three categories, from clean supply water to grossly contaminated water. The category decides what can be dried and what must be removed.

The crew also takes initial moisture readings with a meter and a thermal camera. Those readings become the baseline for the whole job.

Stage three: stopping the source and protecting the space.

If the water is still running, a plumber or the crew shuts it off. Furniture is lifted or moved, wet contents are sorted and floors may be protected from further tracking. In contaminated jobs the crew sets containment so other rooms stay clean.

Stage four: water extraction.

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water first. Carpet, pad and wood floors get extracted with specialized wands. The faster the bulk water leaves, the less drying work remains. The water extraction page explains the equipment in more depth.

Stage five: removal of materials that cannot be dried.

Some materials are not worth saving. Saturated insulation, carpet pad and soft drywall are common examples. Removing them lowers the moisture load and reduces the chance of mold. The crew should explain each removal and photograph it before disposal.

Stage six: drying and dehumidification.

Air movers push dry air across surfaces, and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Specialty tools dry wall cavities and floors from inside. Crews return each day to take readings and move equipment, because drying is not a set-it-and-forget-it job. The structural drying page lists each piece of equipment and its role.

Stage seven: cleaning and sanitizing.

Once the structure is drying, surfaces are cleaned and, if needed, treated with antimicrobial products. In sewage or flood jobs this step is longer and follows stricter handling rules. Contents are cleaned, stored or discarded depending on their condition.

Stage eight: documentation and handoff.

The crew gives you readings, photos and a record of what was removed. Your insurer may ask for that file. After mitigation, rebuilding begins with new drywall, flooring and paint. In some cases the same company does both, and in others a general contractor takes over.

A Sand Springs note on rebuild permits.

The City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office issues building and trade permits, and alterations involving load-bearing walls or electrical, plumbing or mechanical systems generally need one. Mitigation alone rarely needs a permit, while repairs can.

If your home was built decades ago, tell the crew before any cutting, because older materials sometimes need testing first.

Signs a remediation job is going well.

  • The crew explains the category of water and shows readings.
  • Removals are photographed and explained.
  • Equipment placement changes as readings change.
  • You receive a written scope before work begins.
  • The final visit includes a readings check, not a calendar date.

What you can do during the process.

Stay out of the work area unless the crew says it is safe. Keep pets and children away from equipment and cords, and do not turn equipment off to save noise or power. Keep a list of questions, because crews are happy to explain readings when they have your attention.

If you have to be out of the house during the work, tell the crew how to reach you and where valuables are stored. Moving irreplaceable items out before the crew arrives avoids confusion and gives the crew clear access.

When the job grows.

Sometimes opening a wall or lifting a floor shows more water than expected. A good crew pauses, shows you the finding and explains how the plan changes. A change in scope is normal when hidden moisture appears, and it should always be explained before additional work is done.

What to do before the crew arrives.

  • Stop the water at its source if you safely can.
  • Keep out of rooms where water touches outlets.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Lift small valuables and papers off wet surfaces.
  • Have your insurance details and a contact number ready.

Remediation ends when the readings match a dry reference, not when the equipment has run for a set number of days.
