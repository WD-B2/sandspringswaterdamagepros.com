Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained in Sand Springs, OK.

What clean, gray and black water mean in practice, and how time can change a category.

Written by the Sand Springs Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(918) 306-8113.
Call now.

When a restoration company says the water is Category 1, 2 or 3, they are describing how contaminated it is. The category drives almost every decision that follows, from what you can keep to what protective gear the crew wears. It is worth understanding before anyone starts work.

The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration.

Category 1: clean water.

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source. A broken supply line, an overflowing sink with clean water and rain through a roof opening are common examples. It poses little risk when it first appears.

Clean water does not stay clean. As it sits, it picks up dirt, bacteria and building materials. After a day or two, Category 1 water can become Category 2.

Category 2: gray water.

Gray water has some contamination that could make people sick. Washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow and water from a toilet bowl without feces are examples. It is not safe to drink and requires careful handling.

Porous materials that stayed wet in gray water are often removed. Carpet pad, insulation and drywall at the lower part of a wall are typical removals.

Category 3: black water.

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated. Sewage, floodwater from rivers or creeks and water from outside that carried soil and chemicals fall here. It can contain bacteria, viruses and parasites.

Category summary.
Category.Examples.Handling.
Category 1: cleanSupply line break, rain through the roofExtract and dry, many materials can be saved.
Category 2: grayWasher or dishwasher dischargeExtract, clean, remove porous materials.
Category 3: blackSewage, river or creek floodRemove porous materials, sanitize, protective gear.

Sewage jobs follow stricter rules. See the sewage backup page for how crews handle them, and the flood page for river or creek water.

Classes: how much water is in the building.

The standard also describes classes, which measure how fast the water is expected to dry based on how much it soaked. Class 1 is the smallest and affects only part of a room. Class 4 involves materials that hold water deeply, such as hardwood, plaster and concrete.

  • Class 1: little absorption, small area.
  • Class 2: carpet and cushion, walls wet part way up.
  • Class 3: water from above, ceilings, walls and floors all saturated.
  • Class 4: deep saturation in low-permeability materials.

Why categories matter for your safety.

Do not try to clean gray or black water with a household wet vacuum. Wear boots and gloves, avoid skin contact and keep children and pets away. Health questions belong to a physician. A professional crew uses containment, protective gear and disinfectants suited to the category.

Categories in a Sand Springs context.

Floodwater is Category 3. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers released about 275,000 cubic feet per second from Keystone Dam in May 2019, and any building that took on river or creek water in an event like that is a Category 3 job.

The City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office notes that its only local plumbing amendment requires backwater valves on all building drain sewer connections. A working valve helps reduce the risk of Category 3 sewer water entering a home.

What the category means for insurance.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department says standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood and backed-up drains and sewers, which are often Category 3. A clean supply line break is commonly covered. The category does not decide coverage, but it points to the likely cause.

How time and temperature change a category.

Categories are not fixed labels. Warm weather speeds up bacterial growth, so water that begins as Category 1 can reach Category 2 within a day or two in a hot house. Standing water that touches soil, trash or dirty carpet picks up contaminants quickly. Crews reassess the category at every visit and adjust the plan when it changes.

That is one more reason to call quickly. A clean-water job caught within hours is a much simpler job than the same leak found after a long weekend.

Protective gear and what to expect on site.

For Category 2 and 3 jobs, crews wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators suited to the work. They may seal the work area with plastic sheeting, use air scrubbers and bag contaminated material before it leaves the house. You may be asked to stay out of the affected rooms. These steps protect you, the crew and the clean parts of your home.

What to do and what not to do around contaminated water.

  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the affected area.
  • Wear boots, gloves and eye protection if you must enter.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or fan on gray or black water.
  • Do not eat food that touched the water.
  • Wash hands thoroughly after any contact.

When in doubt, treat the water as more contaminated than it looks, and let a crew confirm.
