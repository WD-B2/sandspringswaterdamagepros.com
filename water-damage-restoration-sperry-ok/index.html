Sperry's place on the map.

Sperry is a town in Tulsa County with a 2020 population of 1,115 and ZIP code 74073. It sits about ten miles north of downtown Tulsa and was incorporated in 1920. The Town of Sperry provides water, and Hominy Creek runs near the community.

The dispatch line answers 24/7, so a call at midnight reaches a person, not a recording. Same-day response is offered when a crew is free, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Hominy Creek and wet-weather risk.

Creek valleys collect runoff during heavy rain, and properties near Hominy Creek can see water in garages, crawl spaces and low rooms. If water entered from the outside, the flood damage cleanup page explains contamination handling. A wet crawl space is covered in the crawl space page.

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so check your coverage before the next storm. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for an address.

Take these steps before a crew arrives.

Close the valve.

Shut off the main line if a pipe split or an appliance is leaking.

Kill power if safe.

Do it only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

Move what you can.

Lift papers, electronics and small furniture away from wet floors.

Call your agent.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department advises contacting your agent right away.

Town water and private lines.

In Sperry the Town of Sperry supplies water, so a leak on the street side of the meter belongs to the town and a leak inside the house belongs to the homeowner. Ask the town office where its responsibility ends. A plumber handles anything inside the house, and the restoration crew handles the water.

Rural properties outside town limits may use a different provider, and some outbuildings and barns are on separate lines. Mention those when you call.

Getting to Sperry.

Crews reach Sperry from Sand Springs by way of north Tulsa. Travel time depends on traffic, and the dispatcher gives you a window on the call. The Osage Prairie Trail runs nearby, and the town is served by Sperry Public Schools, which are useful landmarks if you are giving directions.

Properties that are far from the main road may need a gate code or a driveway description, so have those ready.

Older homes, barns and shops around Sperry.

Sperry includes older houses, rural properties and outbuildings. Barns and shops often have dirt or concrete floors and few interior walls, so water spreads differently from a typical house. Tell the dispatcher what the building is made of, how large it is and whether it holds equipment, feed or other items that need to be moved.

If a house has a crawl space, mention it. Wet crawl spaces are common after heavy rain near creeks and can send moisture up into floors.

Insurance notes for Sperry homeowners.

Properties near Hominy Creek or on low ground should ask their agent about flood coverage, since standard policies usually exclude it. Frozen pipe damage may not be covered if the house was left unheated, according to the Oklahoma Insurance Department. Keep the heat on during winter trips.

After any loss, photograph the damage, save the broken part and call your agent promptly. If a well, pump or pressure tank is involved, mention it so the insurer knows what failed.

A short checklist for the call.

  • Street address and nearest crossroad.
  • Water source, if you know it, and whether it is stopped.
  • Which rooms or buildings are wet.
  • Whether the water is clean, from a drain or from outside.
  • A phone number where someone can be reached on site.
