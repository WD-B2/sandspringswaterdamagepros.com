Sewage coming up a drain or toilet right now.

Sewage is an emergency because it is contaminated water that can carry bacteria and viruses. Dispatch runs 24/7, nights and weekends included. While you wait, keep people and pets out of the room, do not run water or flush, and do not try to clean it up with a household wet vacuum.

Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash hands thoroughly after contact. Photograph the backup, and call your insurer and the city or your sewer provider if the main line is involved.

A plumber clears the line and finds the cause. The restoration crew removes the contaminated material and sanitizes the space. These are separate jobs, and the water should be stopped before cleanup begins.

Why sewage is handled differently.

Sewage water is Category 3, which means it is grossly contaminated. Porous materials that touched it, such as carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are normally removed rather than cleaned. Hard surfaces are washed and treated with disinfectants approved for the job.

Crews wear protective gear, bag waste and run air scrubbers when needed. The process follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and it is reasonable to ask how contaminated material will be removed and disposed of.

What the cleanup includes.

  1. Contain the area so contamination does not spread to clean rooms.
  2. Pump or extract the sewage from floors and low areas.
  3. Remove carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that cannot be cleaned.
  4. Clean and disinfect hard surfaces, framing and the subfloor.
  5. Dry the structure and confirm readings before rebuild starts.

Contents are sorted into those that can be cleaned and those that should be discarded. Soft items such as upholstery and mattresses are usually discarded after sewage contact.

Backwater valves and Sand Springs sewer connections.

The City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office notes that its only local plumbing amendment requires backwater valves on all building drain sewer connections. A backwater valve can reduce the chance of sewage backing into a house from the street line, but it needs to be maintained and kept clear to work.

Ask a licensed plumber whether your home has one and whether it is working. If water is entering from a flooded creek or low-lying lot rather than a drain, the basement page or the flood page may apply.

Insurance and sewage backups.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department says standard homeowners policies usually exclude backed-up drains and sewers. Some insurers offer a water backup endorsement.

Read your policy, ask your insurer which coverage applies and document the damage before cleanup. Want your real number? Call (918) 306-8113.

Returning to the space.

After a Category 3 cleanup, the space is not ready to use until surfaces are cleaned, disinfected and dry. Ask the crew to walk you through what was done and which areas are cleared for use. Do not store food, clothing or bedding in the affected room until it has been confirmed clean and dry.

If the backup keeps coming back, have a plumber scope the line with a camera. Tree roots, collapsed pipe and grease buildup are common causes, and fixing the line is the only way to stop a repeat.
