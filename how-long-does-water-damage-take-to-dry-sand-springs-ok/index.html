How Long Does Water Damage Take to Dry in Sand Springs, OK?

Realistic drying times by material, why humidity changes the answer and how to tell when a structure is truly dry.

Written by the Sand Springs Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(918) 306-8113.
Call now.

Everyone asks how long it will take. The answer is that it depends on what got wet, how much and how the building is conditioned. Some rooms are dry in three days. Others need a week or more. What matters is not the day count but the readings.

This guide explains the factors that set the timeline and how a crew decides when equipment can go.

A rough range by material.

General drying expectations.
Material.Typical pace.Why.
Carpet (pad removed)Fast, often a few daysFibers release water easily.
DrywallA few days to a weekPaper face and gypsum hold moisture.
Framing lumberSeveral days to more than a weekWood dries slowly from inside.
Hardwood flooringA week or moreControlled drying avoids splitting.
Concrete slabOften the slowestDense and releases moisture gradually.

These are general patterns, not promises. A crew gives you a plan after taking readings.

What makes drying slower.

  • The water sat for days before drying began.
  • The water was contaminated, which adds removal steps.
  • Walls, floors and ceilings all got wet.
  • Humidity outside is high.
  • The house is closed and hard to condition.
  • Materials such as plaster, concrete or thick hardwood are involved.

Why humidity matters.

Evaporation needs air that can take on moisture. If the air is already full of water, drying stalls. A dehumidifier lowers the moisture in the air so that evaporation can continue, and air movers keep fresh dry air flowing across surfaces.

Oklahoma summers can be very humid, and a house with windows open lets in more moisture than the equipment can remove. A crew keeps doors closed and, in some cases, ducts dry air in.

How a crew knows it is done.

A crew compares the wet materials with a dry reference reading from an unaffected part of the same house. When wet areas match the reference, they are dry. A moisture meter and a thermal camera give the numbers. The structural drying page goes deeper on the equipment and the method.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard for water damage restoration. It is fair to ask whether a company works to it and how completion is documented.

Why stopping early is a mistake.

A wall that feels dry can still hold moisture. If equipment is pulled early, the water left behind can raise mold and odor problems weeks later. EPA guidance says wet materials generally should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Keeping equipment on site until readings match costs more days but saves repairs.

Sand Springs weather and drying.

Spring in northeast Oklahoma is wet. The National Weather Service Tulsa office lists a March precipitation normal of 3.10 inches for 1991 to 2020, and humid days slow drying in buildings with open doors or damaged roofs.

If the roof or windows are broken, covering them is part of drying, because new rain adds water faster than equipment can remove it. See the storm water page for the sequence.

Ways to help the process.

  • Keep exterior doors and windows closed while equipment runs.
  • Do not turn off dehumidifiers or air movers early.
  • Keep pets and children away from equipment and cords.
  • Let the crew move furniture and open cabinets.
  • Ask for readings on each visit.

For wet floors, read about hardwood floors, which dry on their own timeline. Call (918) 306-8113 if you have water now. Neighbors in Tulsa and Mannford get the same approach.

A day-by-day picture of a typical job.

On the first day, the crew extracts water, removes ruined materials and sets equipment. Readings that day are high everywhere. By the second day, surface materials such as carpet and the face of the drywall start to drop. The crew adjusts equipment to concentrate on the slowest areas.

By the third and fourth days, the easy materials are dry and the stubborn ones are still giving up water. Wall cavities, baseboards, thick subfloors and concrete are typically last. The crew removes equipment from rooms that have reached the goal and keeps it on the wettest rooms.

Signs that drying has stalled.

  • Readings stay flat for two visits in a row.
  • The room feels humid even with the dehumidifier running.
  • A musty odor appears or gets stronger.
  • Condensation forms on windows or cold surfaces.
  • One wall reads wetter than the wall next to it.

A stall does not mean failure. It usually means moisture is trapped behind something, such as cabinets, baseboard or a vapor-resistant floor covering. A good crew opens the area up and changes the method.

What to do while you wait for the structure to dry.

  • Do not turn off dehumidifiers or air movers to save power.
  • Keep exterior doors closed and avoid opening windows.
  • Keep pets and children away from equipment.
  • Ask the crew for the readings after each visit.

Drying is done when readings match a dry reference, not when a calendar says so.
