Why kitchens are hard to dry.

A kitchen has cabinets sitting on the floor, plumbing in the walls and appliances that hide leaks. Water gets trapped behind toe kicks, under dishwashers and inside cabinet bases, where it can sit for days. Most kitchen water damage is found late because it is hidden.

Kitchen materials and how water affects them.
Material.How it reacts.Usual outcome.
Particleboard cabinet baseSwells and crumblesOften replaced.
Solid wood cabinetsCan be dried if caught earlyOften saved.
Laminate countertopEdges swell and liftReplaced if the substrate swells.
Stone countertopNot damagedCleaned and reset if removed.
Vinyl or tile floorWater goes under itLifted to dry the subfloor.

Common kitchen leak sources.

  • A dishwasher supply line, drain hose or door seal.
  • A supply line or drain under the sink.
  • A refrigerator ice maker or water line.
  • A pipe inside the wall behind the sink.
  • A failed faucet connection or garbage disposal.

Appliance failures have their own page at appliance leak water damage, and pipe failures in the wall follow the burst pipe response.

Drying cabinets and floors without tearing out the kitchen.

Crews often remove toe kicks and drill small holes so drying air can reach the cabinet bases and subfloor. Injection drying mats push dry air under cabinets and floors. The goal is to dry what can be saved and replace only what cannot.

Ask the crew to show moisture readings at the cabinet base, the subfloor and the wall behind. That tells you whether the kitchen can be dried in place or whether a section has to come out.

Food, dishes and contents.

Items stored in lower cabinets may be wet or contaminated. Discard food that touched water, and wash dishes and cookware that were exposed to anything other than clean supply water. Take photos of damaged items for your insurer before disposing of them.

If the kitchen has hardwood, the hardwood floor page explains what to expect.

Countertops, backsplashes and appliances.

Stone and solid-surface countertops are not harmed by water, but the cabinets under them can be. Laminate countertops swell where the particleboard core gets wet, and the edge may lift. Tile backsplashes can hide water behind them, and grout lines that darken or crack are clues. Refrigerators, ranges and dishwashers should be unplugged and checked before use if water reached their bases.

Gas ranges deserve extra care. If water reached the gas connection or the control board, have the appliance inspected before you light it.

Sinks, faucets and the garbage disposal.

A slow drip from a faucet or a loose drain connection can wet the cabinet floor for weeks. The first sign is often a swollen cabinet bottom or a musty smell when you open the door. Dry the cabinet base, replace damaged material and fix the connection. A new sealant bead at the sink rim also stops water from running behind the counter.

What to move out before the crew arrives.

  • Food, dishes and cookware from lower cabinets.
  • Cleaning products and anything that should not mix with water.
  • Small appliances and electronics.
  • Rugs, mats and loose items on the floor.
  • Anything on the counter near the leak.

According to EPA guidance, wet materials generally should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why cabinet bases and subfloors are opened and checked the same day.
