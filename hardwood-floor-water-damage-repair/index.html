What water does to hardwood and how fast.

Wood absorbs water and swells across the grain. Within hours, boards can start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. If the water stays, the floor can crown or buckle, and the boards may lift off the subfloor.

A walnut or oak floor that cupped after a slow leak is not always ruined. Drying the boards and the subfloor first, then waiting for the wood to settle, saves many floors that would otherwise be torn out.

Cupping, crowning and buckling: three different problems.

Hardwood damage types.
Symptom.What you see.What it usually means.
CuppingBoard edges higher than the centerMoisture came from below or the underside is wetter than the top.
CrowningCenter higher than the edgesOften follows sanding of a cupped floor before it dried.
BucklingBoards lifting off the subfloorSevere or long-lasting moisture, often a full-board failure.
StainingDark or gray patchesWater sat on the finish or tannins reacted in the wood.

Do not sand or refinish a cupped floor until it has been dried and measured. Sanding wet wood just creates a crowned floor once it dries.

Drying hardwood without wrecking it.

Hardwood dries slowly, and drying it too fast can split the boards. Crews use controlled drying with dehumidifiers, air movers and sometimes drying mats that pull moisture through the board and the subfloor. Readings on the boards, the subfloor and a dry reference floor tell the crew when to stop.

The same equipment is described on the structural drying and dehumidification page. Engineered wood and laminate behave differently from solid boards, so tell the dispatcher what type of floor you have.

Where hardwood water damage comes from.

  • A dishwasher, refrigerator ice maker or washing machine line that leaked slowly under the floor.
  • A burst or pinhole pipe in the wall or crawl space under the room.
  • Moisture from a wet crawl space below an older home.
  • An AC condensate line that backed up beside the floor.
  • Storm water that came in under a door or through a window.

Check appliance leak water damage if a dishwasher or fridge line is the likely source, and the crawl space page if the home has a vented crawl space under the floor.

Repair, refinish or replace.

  1. Dry the boards and subfloor until readings match a dry reference.
  2. Wait for the boards to settle, which can take weeks for solid wood.
  3. Sand and refinish if cupping has flattened out and the boards are intact.
  4. Replace boards that are buckled, split or stained through.
  5. Blend the repair into the surrounding floor so the finish matches.

A floor that is flat again may still need refinishing to blend. A restoration crew handles the drying, and a flooring contractor often handles sanding and finish. Ask who does each step so nothing falls between trades.

Cost factors for hardwood water damage.

  • Water categoryClean supply water is easier to dry than gray or contaminated water..
  • Affected areaSquare feet of wet floor and whether the water spread under walls..

Questions to ask a flooring contractor.

  • Has the moisture content of the boards and subfloor been measured?
  • How long will you wait before sanding?
  • Can the finish be blended or does the whole room need refinishing?
  • What happens to the boards if they do not flatten?
  • Who takes responsibility if cupping returns?

A flooring contractor who asks about moisture readings first is a good sign. One who offers to sand tomorrow is not.

What to do while boards are wet.

  • Wipe standing water off the surface right away.
  • Do not seal the floor with plastic or wax, which traps moisture inside.
  • Do not sand or refinish until readings show the boards and subfloor are dry.
  • Remove rugs and mats that hold moisture against the wood.

According to EPA guidance, wet materials generally should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and dense hardwood is one of the materials that rarely meets that window without equipment.
