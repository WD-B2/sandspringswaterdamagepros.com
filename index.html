Water damage restoration for Sand Springs homes and businesses.

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water, drying the structure and repairing what could not be saved. It starts with stopping the source and ends when moisture readings match a dry reference. Sand Springs Water Damage Pros takes calls 24/7 from homeowners, landlords and business owners in Sand Springs, OK and the towns around it.

Most calls come from a burst pipe, a failed appliance line, a roof leak, a sewer backup or storm water that found its way inside. Each one is handled differently, which is why the site has a page for every cause. You can start with flood damage cleanup, water extraction or commercial water damage, or browse the full list below.

Water mitigation first, water damage repair second.

Water mitigation means stopping further damage by pulling out standing water and drying wet materials. Water damage repair is the rebuild that follows, such as new drywall, flooring or paint. Mixing the two up is how people get a quote that includes both and then cannot compare it with another.

What to do while you wait for a crew.

  • Close the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is the source.
  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or the panel.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Lift small valuables and papers off wet floors if it is safe.
  • Call your insurance agent and write down the claim number.

Ask any company which part of the job it is pricing. The structural drying page explains how mitigation is measured, and each service page lists what moves the price before anyone is on site.

Call and describe.

Tell the dispatcher what happened, where the water is and whether it touches electrical panels or appliances.

Why water damage in Sand Springs has its own risks.

Sand Springs sits on the Arkansas River just west of Tulsa, with Keystone Lake and Keystone Dam upstream. In May 2019 the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers released about 275,000 cubic feet per second from Keystone Dam, the highest release since 1986. A levee of roughly 20 miles protects about 10,000 people between Sand Springs and Tulsa, and it is owned by Tulsa County Drainage District 12.

Inside the city, Fisher Creek and Anderson Creek drain neighborhoods such as the Meadow Valley subdivision, where the city's floodplain plan notes repetitive loss structures remain. Whether you live near Prattville, the Box District, The Triangle or Case Community Park, the first question is the same: where did the water come from?

None of that means every house floods. It means the cause of your water matters, and the right page on this site depends on it. Questions about floodplains and permits go to the City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office at City Hall.

Document the damage for your insurer.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department advises homeowners to contact their agent right away, photograph the damage and make reasonable temporary repairs. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, interior stormwater without roof or wall damage and backed-up drains or sewers.

Take photos before anything is moved, keep broken parts and save invoices. We keep moisture readings and drying logs for every job, and you can hand them to your adjuster along with any repair estimate.
