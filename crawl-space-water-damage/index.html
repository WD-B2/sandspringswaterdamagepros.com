What is going on under the house.

A crawl space is out of sight, which is why water often sits there for weeks. Signs include a musty smell inside the house, cupping floors, sagging insulation and a cold, damp feel at floor level. If you see standing water or damp dirt, the cause is either a plumbing leak or water coming in from outside.

Irrigation and plumbing leaks.

A leaking irrigation valve or a broken drain line under the house can flood a crawl space even when no rain has fallen. Shut off the sprinkler system and the water main if you suspect a pipe, and watch the meter for movement with every fixture off.

Pipes in the crawl space are repaired by a plumber, as covered on the burst pipe page. If there are wet floors above, the hardwood floor page shows what the boards need.

What a crawl space dry-out involves.

  1. Extract standing water with a small pump or wet vacuum.
  2. Remove wet insulation that has fallen or soaked through.
  3. Set air movers and a dehumidifier sized for the space.
  4. Clean and treat framing that shows mold or heavy staining.
  5. Measure joists and subfloor moisture until they match a dry reference.

Working under a house is cramped and can involve electrical, gas and insect hazards. Crews use protective gear and lighting, and homeowners should not crawl in to inspect wet areas near wiring.

Fix the cause or the water returns.

Drying a crawl space does not stop water from entering. Check that gutters empty away from the foundation, that soil slopes away from the house and that vents and any vapor barrier are intact. A drainage or foundation contractor handles grading and drainage work, and the restoration crew handles the water damage.

Sand Springs sits on hilly terrain near the Arkansas River and Keystone Lake, and lots on slopes or near creeks can collect runoff differently from lots on flat ground. The city Neighborhood Services office can answer floodplain and drainage questions for a specific address.

Safety before anything else.

A crawl space can hide live wiring, gas lines, sharp debris and animals. Standing water near electrical lines is a shock hazard, so power to the area should be shut off at the panel before anyone enters. Wear gloves, a respirator rated for dust and mold and knee protection, and tell someone where you are.

If the crawl space has been wet for a long time, expect a damp, earthy smell and possibly visible mold on joists. Heavy growth is a remediation job, and a professional should decide whether the area is safe to enter.

Insulation, vapor barriers and ventilation.

Fiberglass insulation that falls and soaks becomes a sponge, and a torn vapor barrier lets ground moisture rise. Whether to replace insulation, repair the barrier or add mechanical ventilation depends on the house. Ask a qualified contractor to explain the options and the reasons for each one.

What to do before anyone goes under the house.

  • Shut off power to the crawl space if you can reach the panel safely.
  • Do not enter if water is deep, if you smell gas or if you see exposed wiring.
  • Do not run a household fan or heater into the space.
  • Shut off the irrigation system and the main water valve if you suspect a pipe.

According to EPA guidance on moisture and mold, controlling moisture is the key to controlling mold, which is why standing water under a house is dried and its cause fixed.
