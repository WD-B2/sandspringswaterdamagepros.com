Wall still damp, floor still soft, house smells musty?

If water was cleaned up days ago and the wall still feels cool, the baseboard has swelled or the room smells damp, the water is still inside the structure. That is a structural drying job, which uses air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture meters to pull trapped moisture out of framing, subfloor and drywall. This page explains how that process works.

Moisture mapping: finding the water you cannot see.

A moisture map starts with a pin or pinless moisture meter that reads wood, drywall and concrete, and a thermal camera that shows cool wet areas on walls and ceilings. The crew records the readings room by room and compares them with dry reference readings from an unaffected part of the same house. Those numbers become the drying goal.

Mapping repeats on every visit. A wet area that shrinks and a reading that falls are how the crew knows the plan is working, and a flat reading shows where equipment needs to move.

The equipment and what each piece does.

Drying equipment and its job.
Equipment.What it does.Where it goes.
Axial air moverMoves air across wet surfaces to speed evaporationLow along walls and across floors.
LGR dehumidifierPulls moisture out of the air so evaporation can continueIn the room, doors closed, ducted if needed.
Injection mat or drying panelPushes dry air into wall cavities and under floorsAgainst wet walls or floor surfaces.
Moisture meter and thermal cameraMeasures and finds moistureUsed on every visit.

Air movers without a dehumidifier just push humid air around. A dehumidifier without air movers dries the air but not the wall. The two work as a pair, and the crew adjusts both when the readings change.

Water mitigation: the insurance and restoration term.

Water mitigation means stopping further damage by removing water and drying the structure, usually before any repair begins. Insurers often ask for mitigation records because they show what was done, when and what the readings were. Keep the daily logs and photos, and give them to your insurer along with any repair estimate.

Drying after different kinds of water.

Structural drying follows whichever event wet the building. After a burst pipe, drying often focuses on a few rooms and the wall the pipe ran through. After a flood, porous materials may come out first and the structure dries after cleaning and treatment. A wet carpet and pad may be dried in place or removed depending on the water category.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and it is fair to ask any company whether its crews work to it and how drying completion is documented.

When drying uncovers something bigger.

Sometimes opening a wall reveals rot, mold or a structural issue. The City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office issues building and trade permits, and its page says new construction or alterations involving load-bearing walls generally need one. If your framing needs repair, ask the city whether a permit applies before work starts.

How long does drying take.

Most affected rooms need several days of active drying, and thick materials such as hardwood, plaster and concrete can need longer. The crew finishes when readings match the dry reference, not when a certain number of days have passed. Plan for monitoring visits during the process.

What to do while equipment is running.

  • Keep exterior doors and windows closed so the dehumidifiers work on the room, not the outdoors.
  • Do not unplug equipment or turn it off to save noise.
  • Keep children and pets away from cords and hot exhaust.
  • Do not move equipment on your own, because placement is part of the drying plan.
