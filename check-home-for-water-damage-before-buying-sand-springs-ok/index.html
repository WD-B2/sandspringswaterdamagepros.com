How to Check a Home for Water Damage Before Buying in Sand Springs, OK.

A walk-through checklist for buyers who want to spot old leaks, wet crawl spaces and flood risk before closing.

Written by the Sand Springs Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(918) 306-8113.
Call now.

Water damage is one of the most expensive problems to discover after closing. A fresh coat of paint can hide a stain, and a dry day can hide a wet crawl space. A careful walk-through, a few questions and some record checks catch most of what a quick look misses.

This checklist is for buyers in Sand Springs and nearby towns. It does not replace a licensed inspector, and it should be used alongside one.

Start outside.

Walk around the house first. Look at where the soil meets the foundation, where downspouts empty and how the ground slopes. Water should move away from the house, not toward it. Cracks in the foundation, damp bricks and mineral stains on the base of the wall are signs that water has been present.

  • Gutters that sag, overflow or empty at the foundation.
  • Soil sloping toward the house.
  • Window wells that are full of debris or standing water.
  • Stains or rust marks on the garage floor or siding.
  • Dead patches of grass near the foundation.

Inside: use your nose and your eyes.

A musty smell is one of the strongest clues. So is a freshly painted patch on an otherwise old ceiling. Look at ceilings, the bases of walls and the corners of rooms. Check under sinks, behind the toilet and around the water heater.

The basement and crawl space.

These spaces tell the truth about a house. Look for water lines on walls, efflorescence, which is a white powder on concrete, and rusted utilities. Sump pumps should be tested and discharge lines should empty away from the house. Wet insulation in a crawl space is a red flag.

Read about basement water damage and crawl space water damage to know what a cleanup involves. A seller who says the basement has never leaked should still be able to explain the sump pump.

Questions to ask the seller.

  1. Has the house ever had water damage, a roof leak or a burst pipe?
  2. Were any insurance claims filed for water, and when?
  3. Who repaired the damage, and is there paperwork?
  4. Has the sump pump or backwater valve ever failed?
  5. Is the home in a mapped floodplain, and is flood insurance required?

In many states sellers must disclose known problems, but disclosure rules vary and do not catch what the seller does not know. Ask for repair receipts and permits.

Flood and permit checks for Sand Springs addresses.

The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for an address. The city's floodplain plan notes that Fisher Creek, Anderson Creek and the Meadow Valley subdivision have a history of repetitive loss, with 20 repetitive loss structures remaining and 17 others below base flood elevation.

The City of Sand Springs Neighborhood Services office issues building and trade permits. Ask whether past remodels, plumbing work or wall changes were permitted, because unpermitted work can hide problems and complicate insurance.

Insurance questions before you close.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department notes that standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood and backed-up drains and sewers. Ask your agent for a quote that includes both before you commit. Also ask whether prior water claims on the property affect your rate.

When to call a pro before closing.

If you find stains, odors or soft floors, ask a licensed inspector to check moisture with a meter and a thermal camera. If a repair is needed, ask for a written scope and negotiate before closing. The structural drying page explains how moisture is measured. Homes in Tulsa and Oakhurst are worth the same checks.

Attic, roof and windows.

Look at the underside of the roof deck if you can reach the attic. Dark stains on the wood, rusted nails and damp insulation point to past or present leaks. Check around chimneys, vents and skylights, because those are the usual weak points. From outside, look for patched shingles and sealant smears that suggest repeated repairs.

Windows deserve a look as well. Soft or discolored wood on the sill, peeling paint at the corners and water marks on the drywall below all show that rain has been getting past the frame.

Plumbing and water heater.

Run the faucets and flush the toilets while you listen for knocking pipes. Look at the supply lines under sinks for corrosion and green crust. Check the age of the water heater from the label and look at the floor around it for rust rings or a pan with water in it. An older tank is a future leak, and the price of replacing it belongs in your budget.

Buy the house, not the paint. Moisture readings and a few questions are cheap compared with a hidden leak.
