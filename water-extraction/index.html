Standing water on the floor right now.

If water is standing on the floor, the next few hours decide how far it travels into drywall, trim and subfloor. The dispatch line answers 24/7, so a call at 2 a.m. gets the same attention as a call at noon. While you wait, stop the source if you can, turn off power to wet areas only when the panel is dry, and move rugs, shoes and boxes off the floor.

If water is near outlets, a water heater or a furnace, leave it alone and call the utility or 911 before the restoration line. Never wade into deep water in a basement with the power on.

Which tool removes which water.

Water extraction is a tool choice problem. Depth, flooring and how fast the level is rising decide what goes on the job first.

Extraction equipment matched to the situation.
Situation.Tool used.Why it fits.
Inches of water over a basement or garage floorSubmersible pumpMoves large volume fast and drains to the outdoors.
Water in wall-to-wall carpetTruck-mounted or portable extractor with a weighted wandPulls water out of the carpet and the pad below it.
Film of water on tile, vinyl or concreteWet vacuum with a squeegee headClears the thin layer left after pumping.
Water trapped under hardwood or in a wall cavityInjection mats and controlled vacuumReaches moisture that surface tools miss.

Removing the water is only the first half.

Extraction takes out the bulk water, but the framing, drywall and subfloor keep holding moisture that a pump cannot reach. That moisture is why the second stage starts the same visit, with air movers to push dry air across wet surfaces and dehumidifiers to pull the humidity out of the room. The full process is laid out on the structural drying and dehumidification page.

EPA mold guidance says wet materials generally should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is the clock the crew is working against after the pump goes quiet.

Reading the water before the pump starts.

A crew checks where the water came from before it touches a hose, because the source sets the safety rules. Clean supply water from a broken line is handled differently from water that rose through a floor drain or entered from outside. Rising outdoor water belongs on the flood damage cleanup page, and a sewer source is covered under sewage backup cleanup.

A burst supply line is the most common clean-water source. The burst pipe page covers where the shutoff valves are and what to do before the crew arrives.

Know whose water line you are standing on.

The City of Sand Springs water system serves about 12,000 customers across roughly 150 square miles, and about 4,500 of those customers live outside the city limits. That matters in an emergency, because a broken service line outside the meter is a utility problem while a broken line inside the house is yours. The city lists an after-hours emergency number on its water service page, and neighbors on rural water districts have their own provider.

Homes in Oakhurst and Mannford can be on different water systems than the City of Sand Springs, so check your last bill to see which provider to call.

What shapes the price of an extraction job.

  • Water categoryClean, gray or contaminated water changes the safety steps and what gets removed..
  • Depth and areaInches of water across a large room need more pumping than a spill in one corner..
  • Materials involvedCarpet and pad, vinyl plank, hardwood and finished drywall all respond differently..
  • Drying daysAfter extraction, equipment stays until moisture readings come back to normal..
  • Rebuild scopeRemoving wet material is one scope and rebuilding it is another..

According to ANSI/IICRC S500, the category of water and the time it has been present guide what can be dried and what is removed, so extraction starts with identifying both.
