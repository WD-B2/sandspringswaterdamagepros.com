Water in the basement tonight: what to do before the crew arrives.

Water in a basement is an emergency because it soaks drywall, framing and stored belongings from the bottom up. The dispatch line is open 24/7, nights and weekends included, so call as soon as you see it. Before anyone goes down the stairs, check whether the water is near the electrical panel, the furnace or the water heater, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

If the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, shut off power to the basement. Carry out what you can lift, keep children and pets upstairs, and take photos from the stairs before anything is moved.

Where basement water usually comes from.

  • A sump pump that stopped, lost power in a storm or cannot keep up with the inflow.
  • Groundwater pushing through the floor joint, cracks or the wall after heavy rain.
  • A window well that filled and spilled over the sill.
  • A supply line, water heater or washing machine hose that failed below the first floor.
  • A floor drain that backed up, which is a contamination problem covered under sewage backup.

If the water is coming from a drain, read sewage backup cleanup before you touch anything. When creek water or river flooding reached the house from outside, the flood damage cleanup page applies instead.

Finished or unfinished: what is at risk.

How basement type changes the cleanup.
Basement type.What the water reaches.Typical result.
Unfinished, bare concrete and framingStored boxes, utilities, bottom platesPump out, clean, dry concrete and lumber.
Partly finished, drywall and carpetCarpet, pad, lower drywall, trimRemove wet carpet and pad, cut drywall, dry framing.
Fully finished, living spaceFlooring, cabinets, furniture, insulationExtensive removal, contents sort, full drying plan.

Insulation behind finished walls and carpet pad soak up water and hold it for days. That is why a basement that looks dry on the surface can keep reading wet behind the paint.

Pumping first, then drying the concrete.

A submersible pump clears the standing water, and extraction units pull what is left from carpet and low spots. Concrete behaves like a sponge and keeps releasing moisture long after the pool is gone, so the crew sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and measures the slab and lower walls with a moisture meter.

Drying depends on air flow, temperature and humidity staying in balance, which the structural drying and dehumidification page explains in more detail. The goal is a basement that reads the same as a dry reference area before anything is rebuilt.

Restoration is not waterproofing.

Cleaning and drying a basement fixes the damage the water caused, not the reason it entered. If water keeps coming in through the wall or floor joint, a waterproofing contractor, a plumber or a drainage specialist handles the cause. Ask each trade what they will do about the source before any finished wall goes back up.

Sand Springs homeowners who are unsure whether their lot sits in a regulated floodplain can ask the city Neighborhood Services office, and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for an address. Hilly lots and creek-side lots drain differently, and neither the map nor the city can promise a basement stays dry.

What moves the price of a basement job.

  • Water categoryClean groundwater, gray water and sewage each follow different handling rules..
  • Affected areaSquare feet of floor, plus how high the water climbed the walls..
  • Materials involvedCarpet, pad, insulation, drywall and stored contents each add a remove or save decision..
  • Drying daysConcrete and below-grade walls dry more slowly than above-grade framing..
  • Rebuild scopePumping and drying are one scope, and refinishing the room is another..

Check your policy before you assume.

The Oklahoma Insurance Department notes that standard homeowners policies usually do not cover flood damage, interior stormwater damage without roof or wall damage, or backed-up drains and sewers. Some policies sell a water backup endorsement separately.

Read your policy, ask your insurer which cause of loss fits, and document the damage before cleanup. Want your real number? Call (918) 306-8113.
