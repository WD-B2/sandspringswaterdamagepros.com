Wet carpet: save it or replace it.

Carpet can often be saved when the water is clean and the drying starts quickly. Whether it can be saved depends on three things: what kind of water it was, how long the carpet stayed wet and whether the pad underneath can be dried. The table below shows how those pieces usually combine.

Carpet and pad decisions by water source.

Why the pad is the real problem.

Carpet fibers dry easily, but the foam pad under the carpet holds water like a sponge. Water trapped in the pad keeps the subfloor wet, which is how mold and odors start. That is why a carpet that feels dry on top can still cause problems a week later.

EPA mold guidance says wet materials generally should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and carpet pad is one of the materials that rarely makes that deadline without equipment.

What an extraction crew does with a soaked carpet.

  1. Extract with a weighted wand that pulls water out of both the carpet and the pad.
  2. Lift the carpet at an edge or corner so air can reach the pad and subfloor.
  3. Cut and remove the pad if it is contaminated or cannot be dried.
  4. Set air movers and a dehumidifier to dry the carpet and subfloor together.
  5. Check the subfloor and tack strips with a moisture meter before the carpet goes back down.

Powerful drying equipment is part of the job. The structural drying and dehumidification page lists what each piece does.

Saving carpet in a Sand Springs home.

Sand Springs sits in Tulsa County, where heavy spring storms and summer humidity both work against wet carpet. A flooded room after a storm needs different handling than a leaking washer hose on a second floor, and the appliance leak water damage page explains the second case. If the water came up from a basement stairwell, the basement page applies.

The crew can often tell during the first visit whether the carpet is worth saving. Ask for the reason behind each replace decision, and photograph the carpet and the water line before anything is removed.

Mold in carpet after water damage.

Mold needs moisture, and wet carpet and pad provide it. Visible fuzzy spots, a musty odor or a dark line along the baseboard point to moisture that has been there for a while. Testing and remediation follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and any health question belongs to a physician.

If the carpet has already been wet for several days, do not try to dry it with fans alone. Call and ask for moisture readings first.

What to do while carpet is wet.

  • Stay off the carpet as much as you can, because walking on it pushes water into the pad.
  • Lift furniture off the carpet and put foil or plastic under the legs.
  • Open interior doors so air moves, but keep the exterior doors shut once equipment is running.
  • Do not use a heat gun or a space heater to dry the carpet.
  • Photograph the wet area before it is cleaned.
