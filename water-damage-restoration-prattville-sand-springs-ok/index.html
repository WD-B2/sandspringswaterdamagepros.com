Where Prattville is.

According to the City of Sand Springs history page, Prattville lay across the Arkansas River from Sand Springs, on land south of the river that Harry Pratt's company sold in the 1920s. Sand Springs annexed it in 1965 after residents had resisted for more than a decade.

Before 1917 a ferry was the only crossing. A wood and iron bridge served from 1917 to 1931, and a concrete bridge opened on February 1, 1931. Today Prattville is part of Sand Springs, and the city page does not mention flooding.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. This page does not say that Prattville floods and does not say that any address is in a flood zone.

Why home age shapes a water damage job.

A house in a subdivision that began between 1926 and 1946 may have original or partly replaced plumbing, plaster or lath walls, wood floors over a crawl space and older wiring. Any of those changes how a wet room should be handled.

According to the EPA, homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint, so sanding, scraping or demolishing painted surfaces after a leak should use lead-safe practices. Older flooring and wall materials can also contain asbestos, and a qualified tester should check them before demolition.

Water problems older homes tend to have.

Slow supply line leaks.

Old pipe joints can seep inside a wall for months before a stain shows. See wall and drywall repair.

Sudden pipe failure.

A split line can put gallons into a room in minutes. See burst pipe cleanup.

Wet crawl spaces.

Moisture under a floor can rise into joists and boards. See crawl space water damage.

Wood floors that cup.

Old hardwood reacts to water by swelling and lifting. See hardwood floor repair.

Safe first steps before anyone arrives.

  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source.
  • Keep out of any room where water touches outlets, a light fixture or the panel, and turn off that circuit only from a dry spot.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and do not run fans through sewage or other dirty water.
  • If the water looks dirty or smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets out.
  • Do not scrape, sand or cut painted plaster or old flooring before it has been tested.
  • Photograph the damage and keep broken parts for your adjuster.

Insurance notes for older homes.

Policies treat a sudden pipe burst differently from a slow seep, and wear and tear is often excluded. According to the Oklahoma Insurance Department, homeowners should contact their agent right away after a loss. Your agent can say how your own policy applies, and this page cannot.

Ask the crew for photographs, moisture readings and a drying log so your adjuster sees what was wet and for how long.
