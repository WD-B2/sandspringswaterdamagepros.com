How an air conditioner ends up leaking inside the house.

An air conditioner pulls humidity out of the air, and all that water has to leave the house through a condensate drain. When the drain clogs, the pan overflows and the water finds the nearest ceiling, wall or floor. In Oklahoma the system runs for months at a time, so a slow leak can wet drywall for weeks before a stain shows.

The most common sources are a clogged drain line, a cracked or rusted drain pan, a frozen evaporator coil that thaws into the pan and a failed float switch that should have shut the unit off. A heating and cooling contractor fixes the cause, while the water damage is a restoration job.

Where the damage shows up.

Attic air handler.

Water drips through insulation and out of a ceiling below, often showing as a brown ring near the hallway.

Closet or utility room unit.

Water soaks the carpet or hardwood at the base of the closet and wicks into the neighboring wall.

Wall-mounted or mini split.

Water runs down the wall under the unit and stains the paint and baseboard.

Window or portable unit.

A tilted or blocked drain pushes water onto the sill and floor.

A condensate line that backs up behind a hardwood floor can cup the boards before anyone sees water. If boards have started to rise at the edges, read the hardwood floor water damage page. A stained ceiling under an attic unit belongs on the ceiling water damage repair page.

Stopping the leak safely.

Turn the thermostat to off so the system stops making more water. If the unit has a drain pan on a ceiling or in a closet, put a wide container under any drip and keep the area clear of outlets. Water that reaches a furnace control board or a wall outlet is an electrical risk, so cut power to that circuit if the panel is dry and call an electrician if you are not sure.

A heating and cooling contractor clears the drain line, replaces a failed pan or float switch and checks the coil. Restoration should begin right after the leak stops so damp insulation and drywall do not sit.

Drying out AC water damage.

AC leak water is usually clean at first but turns gray as it picks up dust, insulation fibers and drain line film. Crews remove wet insulation and loose drywall, place air movers and a dehumidifier and check the wet area with a moisture meter and thermal camera. Hidden water in the wall or ceiling cavity is what causes repeat stains, and the structural drying and dehumidification page describes how it gets dried.

What to do before a crew arrives.

  • Turn the thermostat to off so the system stops making water.
  • Keep water away from outlets, the air handler's electrical box and any wet light fixture.
  • Put a wide container under any drip and move belongings out from under a sagging ceiling.
  • Do not poke a bulging ceiling unless you are ready for the water that will come out.
  • Photograph the stain, the drain pan and the unit.

According to ANSI/IICRC S500, water that starts clean can be reclassified as it picks up contaminants over time, which is why a slow condensate leak is checked before it is called harmless.
