Water heater leaking now: shut it down safely.

A failed tank can release the whole tank, often 40 to 50 gallons, and keep refilling from the supply line. Dispatch runs 24/7, nights and weekends included. Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater, shut off the power at the breaker for an electric unit or set a gas unit to pilot or off, and call us.

Do not touch a wet electric unit or step in water that reaches an outlet. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Why water heaters fail.

  • A corroded tank at the end of its service life, usually after many years.
  • A failed temperature and pressure relief valve that leaks at the discharge pipe.
  • A loose or corroded supply or drain fitting.
  • Sediment buildup that stresses the tank bottom.
  • A leaking pan or drain line that lets water reach the floor.

A small drip from a fitting is a plumbing repair, while a leaking tank body means the unit needs replacement. Either way, the water on the floor needs drying. If the heater is in an attic or upstairs closet, the water often ends up in the ceiling below, which the ceiling repair page covers.

What the crew does.

  1. Confirm the water supply and power are off.
  2. Extract standing water from floors and low spots.
  3. Remove wet insulation, carpet pad and drywall that cannot be dried.
  4. Set air movers and dehumidifiers and take readings.
  5. Document the damage for your insurer and your plumber.

A plumber replaces the heater, and the restoration crew handles the water. Having both on the same day can shorten the time the structure stays wet.

Hot water adds a safety note.

Water from a tank can be hot enough to scald, and steam can hurt people and pets. Let it cool before wading in, and wear shoes with good traction. Gas appliances nearby may have wet controls, so have them checked before relighting.

Sudden tank failures are commonly covered by homeowners insurance, and gradual leaks and maintenance failures often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call (918) 306-8113.

Signs a water heater is about to fail.

  • Rust-colored water from the hot tap.
  • Rumbling or popping sounds as sediment cooks at the bottom.
  • Moisture or rust at the base of the tank or around the fittings.
  • A tank more than ten to fifteen years old.
  • Water pooling in the drain pan.

Replacing a tank before it fails is far cheaper than cleaning up after one. A drain pan with a line to a safe location and a leak sensor on the floor give early warning.

Tankless and attic installations.

Tankless units leak less often, but fittings and condensate lines can still fail. Heaters installed in attics put ceilings and light fixtures at risk, so a pan and drain line are especially important there.

According to ANSI/IICRC S500, clean tank water can degrade to a more contaminated category as it sits, which is another reason to start extraction quickly.
